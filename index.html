Lab 1 · Create

From Thought to Artifact

Think of something that has been on your mind recently.

It could be:

  • An initiative
  • A new idea
  • A trip
  • An event
  • A product or service
  • A personal project
  • Something you may want to propose to someone

You do not need to prepare it. Explain it to Claude naturally.

The prompt
Paste this into Claude 
Something has been on my mind and I want to turn it into something concrete.

I will explain the idea to you in my own words.

First, ask me up to 3 important questions that would help you understand what I am trying to achieve.

Then turn the idea into a polished Artifact that I could show to another person. Choose the format that best fits the idea — for example a concept note, one-page proposal, plan, or short presentation.

Make it clear, concise, visually polished, and useful.

Do not start building until you have asked your questions.
Then make it better

Now improve it by talking to Claude naturally.

For example

  • Make this more concise.
  • Make the recommendation clearer.
  • Remove anything unnecessary.
  • Make this suitable for someone seeing the idea for the first time.
  • Turn this into five slides.
  • Make the visual style more premium.

You do not need a sophisticated prompt. Say what you want changed, the way you would say it to a colleague.

Lab 2 · Personalize

Make Claude Work Like You

Step 1 · The document

Open the Executive Update provided for this lab.

Open Executive Update
Step 2 · Before

Start a new Claude chat.

The prompt 
Summarize this update for me.

Attach or paste the Executive Update and send the prompt. Keep the result.

Step 3 · Your instructions

Now tell Claude how you prefer information to be presented — once, instead of repeating yourself in every prompt.

Add these to your Claude Instructions 
Start with anything that requires my attention or decision.
Keep executive updates concise.
Separate facts, risks, decisions, and recommended actions.
Surface bad news clearly; do not bury it.
Call out missing information and assumptions.
When there are several options, explain the trade-offs.
Use direct, professional language.

You may personalize one or two lines if you want Claude to work differently for you.

Add them in Claude: your name, bottom left › Settings › Account › Instructions for Claude. Save.

Step 4 · After

Start a new chat.

Use exactly the same prompt, and exactly the same Executive Update.

The same prompt 
Summarize this update for me.

What changed — even though the prompt did not?

Lab 3 · Research

Walk Into the Meeting Prepared

Think of something you may realistically need to understand before a meeting.

  • A company you are meeting
  • A potential partner
  • A supplier
  • A market
  • A new technology
  • An industry
  • A competitor
  • A major trend

Use a public or non-sensitive topic during the workshop.

Turn on Research before sending the prompt.

The prompt 
I have an upcoming meeting about [COMPANY / TOPIC / MARKET].

Before you research, ask me:
1. What is the purpose of the meeting?
2. Who am I meeting, or what type of audience is involved?
3. What decision, outcome, or understanding do I want from the meeting?

Then use Research to prepare me.

Create a concise executive meeting brief covering:
- What I need to understand first
- What has changed recently
- The 5 most important things I should know
- Important numbers and facts
- Relevant opportunities
- Risks or concerns
- What may not be obvious
- Different perspectives or uncertainties where relevant
- 5 intelligent questions worth asking in the meeting

Use recent, credible sources. Cite important factual claims. Clearly distinguish verified facts from interpretation.

End with a section titled: "If you only remember three things"

Keep the final brief practical and concise.
Use fictional, public, or non-sensitive information during the workshop. Follow your organization’s policies when working with internal information.
